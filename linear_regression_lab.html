The line is fitted using all cars. Moving one observation changes the training data and can change the slope and R².

What does a negative slope mean?

A fitted slope of −$0.18 per mile would mean that increasing mileage by 1,000 miles is associated with about $180 less in the model's predicted price. It does not prove mileage causes that exact price change.

Practice

Move a high-mileage car above the line. Does the slope become less negative? Does R² change? Predict the price of a car with 100,000 miles.

Why use a predicted-versus-actual chart?

With multiple features, there is no single line that captures the entire model on a simple x–y scatterplot. The green diagonal instead marks perfect predictions. Vertical gaps show dollar errors.

Practice

Uncheck bedrooms and age, then add them back. Did R² improve on these training examples? Change only house age in the sliders. Why does the estimated price change?

A higher-degree model can fit these training points more closely. That does not guarantee better predictions on new students.

Linear versus polynomial

Degree 1 is a straight line. Degree 2 adds a squared-hours term, so the fitted shape can bend. Degree 3 adds a cubic term and is more flexible.

Practice

Compare R² for degrees 1, 2, and 3. Which shape seems to fit these observed scores? Move one student's score far away and compare the results again.
